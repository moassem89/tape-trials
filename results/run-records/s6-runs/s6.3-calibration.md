# s6.3 — Calibration: per-cell record (condition C8)

C8 was authored after the plan, because calibration is not recoverable from the
runs already on record: no confidence was elicited in any earlier condition and
no logprobs were retained, so any Brier score computed from those files would be
scoring a probability nobody produced. Adding a condition post-plan is an
unreviewed autonomous decision; it is recorded in `report.md`.

Each cell runs two confidence signals over the same rows behind one weight load:

- **verbalized** — one greedy pass (temperature 0, top_p 1, max_tokens 16384)
  reading `P6_confidence.txt`, which is byte-identical to the frozen template
  above the output contract and differs only in asking for a stated probability
  alongside the answer. The runner refuses to start if the two templates hash
  the same.
- **self-consistency** — k=5 samples at temperature 0.8 / top_p 0.95 with fixed
  seeds; the confidence is the share of samples agreeing with the modal answer.

Rows are the same fixed-seed stratified 15% subsample C5 uses
(`frac=0.15`, `seed=tape-trials-c5`): 181 rows over 46 programs. Using C5's rows
is deliberate — it makes the verbalized arm directly comparable to C5's greedy
passes on identical inputs, which is what turns the accuracy cost below into a
controlled reading rather than an impression.

## What the 14B-R cell says

**Stated confidence is close to useless on this task.** Every one of the 179
parsed replies landed in the top confidence bin, mean 0.9989, against an
accuracy of 0.8011 — a 19.4-point overconfidence gap whose interval
(0.1014–0.2972) excludes zero. AUROC 0.5216 is a coin flip: the number the model
attaches to an answer does not separate its right answers from its wrong ones.
The Murphy decomposition puts resolution at exactly 0.0, which is the same fact
stated structurally, and the Brier score (0.19447) is *worse* than the constant
predictor that ignores the question and always says 0.8011 (0.1573). The
risk-coverage curve never rises: restricting to the model's most confident tenth
gives 0.7222 accuracy, below the 0.8011 it gets on everything.

**Sampling agreement is a good signal on the same rows.** Majority-of-5 at
temperature 0.8 scores 0.8508 with Brier 0.09392, beating its own base-rate
predictor (0.12692), and AUROC 0.925 means the agreement rate separates correct
from incorrect answers well. It is *under*-confident rather than over
(gap −0.1293), which is the easier error to correct, and it discriminates at
five distinct values with a mean of 2.315 distinct answers per row. Answering
only the half of rows where the five samples agree most gives 0.9889 accuracy.

**The two signals are unrelated**, correlating at 0.0146. Whatever the model
reports as certainty is not what its own sampling variability measures.

## The cost of asking for a confidence statement

The verbalized arm and C5's five greedy passes (`652a33fd`) differ in exactly one
thing: the prompt. Same weights, same 181 rows, same greedy decoding, same token
ceiling, same card. C5 scored 0.9613–0.9890 exact_match. The verbalized arm
scored **0.8011** — roughly 16 points lower for adding a request to state a
probability. Mean reply length was 766 tokens here against 16k available, and
every reply finished on `stop` rather than the ceiling, so truncation does not
explain it.

Two consequences, both of which belong in the paper:

1. C8's accuracies are **not** comparable to the study's headline ladder. They
   are accuracies *within the confidence-eliciting condition*, and the ladder's
   readings stay the frozen-prompt ones.
2. The cost itself is a result. It is a second instance of the wording
   sensitivity C2 measures, on the largest rung, and it is much bigger than
   any of C2's five semantics-preserving rewordings on the same model.

The self-consistency arm shares the frozen contract, so its 0.8508 is not
directly a frozen-prompt reading either: it is majority-of-5 at temperature 0.8,
where the ladder reading is greedy.

Resolution is 0.0 at five decimal places on all three rungs, which is the whole story
in one number: the model emits essentially one confidence value regardless of the row.
The 14B rung emits literally one — a single distinct value across 181 rows, which is
why its AUROC is exactly 0.5 with a degenerate interval. Brier skill is negative
everywhere, so on all three rungs the stated probability is a *worse* forecast than

3B's AUROC interval [0.5316, 0.5901] does technically exclude chance, which is why the

on stated confidence at 3B is worse than not selecting. The same test at 14B gives

#### The ceiling explanation is dead

One cell could not distinguish "this rung had separable rows and said nothing useful
about them" from "this rung's wrong rows are not separable from its right ones by
anything." `exec_steps` — the length of the reference execution, computed by the
grader and never shown to the model — settles it as a difficulty reference:

| Rung | difficulty-ref AUROC [95%] | pool separable | signal available, unreported |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3B | 0.7894 [0.5186, 0.9903] | yes | no (its own CI clears chance) |
| 14B | 0.8065 [0.6552, 0.9144] | yes | **yes** |
| 14B-R | 0.6307 [0.5067, 0.7448] | yes | **yes** |

All three pools are separable: a statistic the model never sees predicts its own
correctness well above chance on every rung. Two of the three then fail to report any
of it. The job's rolled-up reading is `n_rungs_with_separable_pool: 3`,
`n_rungs_signal_available_but_unreported: 2`. So the near-chance verbalized AUROC is
not an artifact of a rung with too few wrong rows to rank — the original innocent
explanation for the 14B-R reading — and it is not an artifact of a flat pool either.
**The elicitation is what failed.**

That holds across an eight-fold accuracy range. 14B-R gets 80% of these rows right and
3B gets 7%, and both state near-certainty on essentially every row.

#### What this answers

Calibration on this task is not poorly calibrated confidence; it is the absence of any
confidence signal to calibrate. Three rungs spanning 7% to 80% accuracy all state
near-certainty on every row, all score worse than the base rate as forecasters, and
two of them demonstrably had separable difficulty available to report. The usable
signal is behavioural rather than stated: five samples and an agreement count. And
asking for the statement is not free — it costs the one rung that could do the work
18 points of accuracy.
