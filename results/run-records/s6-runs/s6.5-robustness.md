## What the two completed cells say so far

**Wording moves the score well past serving noise, and more so on the smaller
rung.** The 14B reasoning model spans 4.6 points across five semantics-preserving
rewordings; the 7B reasoning model spans **13.5** (0.7773 on the tutorial
wording, 0.9118 on the inverted one), against a serving-noise floor of 2.8
points. The floor was measured on 14B-R, not on 7B-R, so the 7B comparison
borrows a floor from a different rung — a caveat the H5 job carries, and one
reason WAVES row 6 puts a second C5 cell on the plain 14B rung.

**The frozen prompt is not a favourable wording.** It is the *worst* of the five
on 14B-R (0.9454 against a 0.9916 best) and third of five on 7B-R. Every
headline number in this study reads that template, so the ladder understates
both reasoning rungs rather than flattering them. Worth stating plainly in the
paper: the benchmark is not tuned to make its models look good.

**Both rungs agree on which wordings are easy.** P5_inverted ranks first and
P2_reference_card second on both, and the between-rung ordering survives every
wording (14B-R's worst, 0.9454, still clears 7B-R's best, 0.9118). That is one
adjacent pair holding under rewording, not the H5 result: the ordering test
needs the whole matrix, and the pairs at risk are the close ones lower down the
ladder.

## 2026-08-26T00:15Z — two dead cells, one stack dump, and a mechanism

This entry closes out the two jobs queued on 2026-08-23 and records what they
cost, what they produced, and why the project sat idle for three days afterwards.

**The idle gap, and whose fault it was.** The 2026-08-23 run queued `a483488c` and
`c6f1cff2` and then ended without writing `.pending-jobs.json`. The backend poller
wakes a project from that marker, so with no marker naming outstanding jobs it read
the project as having nothing in flight and stopped. Both jobs ran to their ends on
the machines, and nothing burned compute in the interim, but nothing advanced
either. Three and a half days lost to a missing file. The claim marker is now
written **before** the first submit rather than after the batch, which is the whole
point of the `"queueing": true` state.

**`a483488c` — WAVES row 4R, C2 paraphrase at plain 14B, 88.3 GPU-min, no result.**
Fourth attempt at this cell, fourth freeze at the vLLM `LLM(...)` constructor. It is
also the single most valuable job this project has run, because the stall handoff
added at 2026-08-22T23:50Z fired on its first outing and captured what five earlier
freezes did not.

The in-process guard wrote its `STALL` sentinel into `SUPERVISE_DUMP_DIR` instead of
calling `os._exit`, `supervise.sh` picked it up and ran `py-spy dump --native` over
the entire process tree, echoed the frames into the job log, and only then reaped
everything. What the frames show:

- the launcher blocked inside `LLM.__init__` at `vllm/entrypoints/llm.py:339`, with
  `enable_multiprocessing: True` on the frame above it;
- a separate process, PID 2437, named `VLLM::EngineCore`, holding **40,973 MiB of
  the card's 46,068** — at `gpu_memory_utilization=0.90` that is the whole budget,
  meaning 29.5 GB of bf16 weights plus a fully allocated KV cache;
- **GPU-Util 0%**, so nothing anywhere was computing;
- every thread in both processes `state=S`, parked on `futex_do_wait` or `ep_poll`;
- about eight repeated sets of `pt_nccl_watchdg`, `pt_nccl_heartbt`,
  `gloo_tcp_loop` and `pt_gloo_runloop`, plus `pt_tcpstore_uv`, in a job running at
  `tensor_parallel_size=1`.

The child had finished building and gone idle. The launcher never learned it. That
is a lost handshake, not a CUDA fault, not a kernel, not a card, and it retires two
candidates that needed the constructor to still be working: weight loading and
KV-cache profiling had both completed. Full reading in
`../tasks/_shared/image-baseline.md`, section 2026-08-26.

**How the 88.3 was derived, and its uncertainty.** Job metadata was thin, so the
charge is reconciled two ways rather than estimated. The platform meter's residual
across the window is 334 − 245.7 = 88.3. Independently, the wall-clock floor is
57.8 min of observed run plus the ~28-minute teardown precedent set by `b16db17a`,
giving about 86. The upper bound, if the meter bills continuously to teardown, is
107.3. The 19 GPU-min of spread is recorded here rather than smoothed away.

**`c6f1cff2` — WAVES row 7, C2 paraphrase at 0.5B on A10G, 3.8 GPU-min, cause
unknown.** Reported COMPLETE with progress 0, no task logs, no artifacts, after
3m47s. That is shorter than the cell's own `pip install -q vllm transformers` step,
so the harness never started and **this is not the engine freeze**. The cluster was
torn down before the machine log could be read. Recorded as an unexplained fast exit
rather than folded into the hang story, because guessing here would corrupt the one
diagnosis this project has. The cell's single permitted retry is spent.

**Both cells are unqueued and stay unqueued.** The operator's rule is one watchdog
kill per cell, then stop and ask. Rows 3R, 4R and 7 have each spent theirs. Between
them they cost roughly 240 GPU-min and produced exactly one thing of value, which is
the dump.

**A serving-configuration change is adopted, and its control is running.**
`VLLM_ENABLE_V1_MULTIPROCESSING=0` removes the mechanism the dump photographs: no
child process, no ZMQ ready handshake, no fork of an interpreter that already owns
CUDA and gloo threads, no dependence on how the node's own hostname resolves. The
knob was checked against the installed vLLM 0.27.1 source rather than assumed, since
a flag vLLM does not recognise looks exactly like no flag for forty-five minutes.

Per the operator's standing note on mid-study serving changes, an already-measured
neighbouring cell runs first under the new setting: WAVES row OC, an exact repeat of
`cbc1215f` (C8 calibration, 14B-R, 181 rows, aws L40S, 84.6 GPU-min) with the
mitigation as the only change. **Nothing else queues until it lands.** If the
calibration numbers hold, rows 5, 6, 8, 9, 10 and 11 run under the mitigation. If
they move, or if it hangs, the mitigation is dead and s6.3 and s6.5 close with the
gaps recorded.

**Two narrower mitigations were deliberately not used**, so that one variable is
under test: `VLLM_WORKER_MULTIPROC_METHOD=spawn` and
`VLLM_LOOPBACK_IP=127.0.0.1`. Both fit the same dump; both stay on the shelf.

**A latent break found while wiring the mitigation in.** `verify_shared.py` was
already failing before any of today's edits: `start_stall_watchdog` in
